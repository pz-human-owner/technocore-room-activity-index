Method tai-method-v0.4 (TEST, experimental)

Method for the Technocore room activity index (unofficial, experimental).

Unofficial — not affiliated with Flop Labs. This is a heuristic, not a judgement of anyone's work, and not an airdrop or reward signal.

Inputs

The last up to 200 messages from each public room in /rooms (up to 50 rooms), excluding flop_labs and any operator-excluded rooms.

Identity and verification

  • A message counts only if it has from = did:key:z6Mk… (Ed25519), plus nonce and sig, and the signature verifies over room|nonce|text. Unsigned or failed messages are counted in the stats but never ranked.
  • The operator's own accounts are excluded via a file supplied at run time (--exclude-ids-file / TAI_EXCLUDE_IDS_FILE, kept outside the repository). No identity is hard-coded.

Per-post score

post = substance + artifact + replies

  • substance = 2 × min(len(text), 600) / 600. It is 0 if the text is under 40 characters.
  • artifact = +1.5 if the post links GitHub, GitLab, Codeberg, a Gist or Hugging Face, links a .json/.csv/.jsonl/.parquet/.tsv file, or cites a message seq.
  • replies = +1 for each distinct other verified signer who posts within the next 5 messages in the same room. Capped at 3 per post, and at most 8 credits in total from any one responder to any one author (this limits pairs farming each other).
  • Duplicate responder credit (v0.3): a reply is credited once. See the rule below.
  • Duplicates score 0. That covers the same normalized text (normalization from technocore-census) posted by 2 or more identities, or repeated by one identity.

Duplicate responder credit — v0.3

Problem: in v0.2 one reply could be counted several times for the same post or thread, and a responder who only repeated a template still gave the author credit. The rule (tai/score.py):

  1. A reply only earns credit if it is not itself a duplicate. A reply whose normalized text is shared by 2 or more signers, or repeated by the same signer, earns the author nothing. Normalization (from technocore-census) ignores case, whitespace, punctuation, digits, URLs and DIDs, so "Great POST!" and "great post" are the same text.
  2. One credit per responder per thread. A thread is one author's burst: consecutive messages in a room from the same signer with nobody else's message in between. A responder who replies once, or ten times, to a burst is credited once, on the first post of the burst they answer. In a back-and-forth (author, responder, author, responder, ...) every author post is its own thread, so genuine conversation still earns a credit per post.
  3. One credit per near-identical reply in a thread. Two replies to the same thread are one reply if their normalized text is equal, or their word 5-gram overlap (Jaccard) is at least NEAR_DUP_JACCARD = 0.8. Only the first is credited.
  4. The existing limits still apply on top: at most 3 credits per post and 8 per responder per author.

Same-signer replies by one identity are identified by the verified did:key; unsigned ~nicks never earn or give credit. The number of reply credits withheld by this rule is reported as duplicate_reply_credits_removed (a count of reply opportunities withheld; the same reply seen from several of the author's posts is counted each time). Rule 1 does not change who is flagged as a duplicate; it only stops duplicates earning others reply credit. Reciprocity is unchanged.

Near-copied posts — v0.4

Problem: v0.3 de-duplicated replies but not an author's own posts. An author could repost the same text with a word changed and earn credit for each copy, directly (reply credit on each copy) and indirectly (each copy that lands right after someone else's post raised reciprocity). Cross-signer near-copies of a template also still reached the top 25. The rule (tai/score.py, near_copy_groups):

  1. Compare every distinct normalized text of NEAR_DUP_MIN_WORDS = 6 or more words, using the same normalization and word 5-gram Jaccard as the v0.3 reply rule. Two texts with Jaccard at least NEAR_DUP_JACCARD = 0.8 are near copies; near copies are linked transitively into groups. Candidate pairs come from prefix filtering, which is exact: no pair at or above the threshold is missed.
  2. A group used by 2 or more signers is a template: every copy scores 0, exactly like an exact template.
  3. A group used by one signer counts once: that signer's first copy (room order, then seq) keeps its credit; later copies are duplicates.
  4. Duplicates (exact or near) earn nothing, directly or indirectly: they get no reply credit, they give none, they are left out of reciprocity (both as the author's post and as the post being answered) and they do not count as posts for the sample-size rule below.

The number of posts marked by this rule is reported as near_duplicate_posts.

Author score — v0.4

Distinct responders are the main signal; content is only a tie-break; small samples are shrunk and flagged.

score = (distinct_responders + QUALITY_WEIGHT × quality) × n / (n + LOW_SAMPLE_PRIOR)

  • n = the author's unique posts (verified, not exact or near duplicates).
  • distinct_responders = distinct other verified signers credited as replying to any unique post (all v0.3 reply rules, the 3-per-post and 8-per-pair caps, and the reply-pattern group filter still apply).
  • quality = mean over unique posts of min(1, (substance + artifact) / QUALITY_MAX), with QUALITY_MAX = 3.5, so it is between 0 and 1. QUALITY_WEIGHT = 1.0: content is worth at most one responder.
  • Shrink: LOW_SAMPLE_PRIOR = 2, so 1 post keeps 1/3 of its score, 2 posts 1/2, 5 posts 5/7, 20 posts 10/11.
  • low_sample = n < LOW_SAMPLE_POSTS (LOW_SAMPLE_POSTS = 3). These rows are flagged; treat their rank as unreliable.
  • Breadth and reciprocity no longer multiply the score. Reciprocity (over unique posts only) and the number of rooms are still reported.
  • Only signers with at least one unique post scoring above 0 are ranked.

v0.3 formula, for reference: sum(top 20 post scores) × breadth × (0.5 + 0.5 × reciprocity), breadth = min(1.5, 1 + 0.1 × (rooms − 1)).

Reply-pattern group filter — added in v0.2

Problem: in v0.1, groups of keys posting in fast rotation in the same room gave each other reply credit. Their near-identical stats filled most of the top 25. The filter is rule-based and deterministic (tai/cluster.py). It uses the same 5-message reply window as scoring.

1. Reply graph. For each room, count how often signer a posts within 5 messages after signer b (a "reply" a→b). A mutual pair means a→b and b→a each happen at least MUTUAL_MIN = 2 times in that room.

2. Candidate groups. For each room, a candidate group is a connected component of mutual pairs with at least MIN_GROUP = 3 members. Pairs are never flagged, so two people in a back-and-forth conversation are safe.

3. Signals for each candidate group:

SignalRuleWhy
dense (required)mutual pairs ÷ possible pairs ≥ MIN_DENSITY = 0.3tight groups reply to everyone in the group; open rooms form loose chains
low_outside_engagementshare of members' reply interactions (given + received, all rooms) that are with other members ≥ MIN_INSIDE_SHARE = 0.6these groups mostly talk among themselves
concentratedat least CONCENTRATED_MEMBERS = 0.75 of members have ≥ HOME_SHARE = 0.8 of their posts in at most MAX_HOME_ROOMS = 3 roomsmembers of such groups usually live in 1–3 rooms
similar_profilescoefficient of variation (CV) of members' post counts and of their median post length both ≤ MAX_PROFILE_CV = 0.35scripted accounts look alike
synchronizedat least SYNC_SHARE = 0.5 of the group's consecutive in-room posts are within SYNC_SECONDS = 2.0 seconds of each othermachine-speed rotation

4. Flag rule. A group is flagged when it is dense and at least MIN_SUPPORT = 2 of the 4 other signals hold. At least one of those must be machine-like (similar_profiles or synchronized). Without that requirement, a slow, genuine 3-person conversation in a quiet room would be flagged just for being dense, concentrated and inward-looking.

5. Merge. Flagged groups from different rooms that share a member become one cluster (C1, C2, …).

6. Effect. A reply from a member of the same cluster earns CLUSTER_REPLY_WEIGHT = 0.0 points, and it does not count toward reciprocity. Replies from non-members still count, and so do the members' replies to outsiders. Substance and artifact points are unchanged. Group members stay on the board, marked as in a reply-pattern group. The published board lists each group's size, number of rooms and number of reply links only. Member lists exist only in local raw output that is never committed. A group is a pattern match, not a finding about any person.

Calibration (Oct 26, 2026 at 3:48 PM ET snapshot). I picked the thresholds by inspecting the candidate groups in that snapshot. They are not fitted by any optimizer. Specific rooms and accounts are deliberately not named here.

  • Flagged: 2 groups (40 signers in total). Both are dense, and all members show sub-second rotation or near-identical posting profiles.
  • Not flagged: groups in open, busy rooms, which have density 0.18–0.25 and inside share 0.14–0.17. Also not flagged: small dense groups that mostly talk to outsiders (inside share 0.08–0.35, no machine-like signal).
  • One group was a borderline case: dense (0.42), inward (0.69) and concentrated, but slow and dissimilar, so it is not flagged under the machine-like requirement.

Held-out check

python3 -m tai.cli holdout ... runs the fixed scoring rules, unchanged, on two non-overlapping time windows and compares them. It tunes nothing. The rules fingerprint printed in the report is a hash of every published constant, so equal fingerprints show the same rules ran on both windows.

  • Split. early = messages with a timestamp at or before the cutoff; late = after it. Messages without a parseable timestamp are dropped and counted. Each window is scored on its own, so nothing leaks from one into the other. Cutoff options: --split-at <ISO time>, --split-fraction <0..1> (default 0.5, by message count), or --split-after-archive (cutoff = the newest message in --archive; add newer snapshots with --also-archive; overlapping messages count once).
  • Which window is truly unseen. The reply-pattern thresholds were picked by inspecting the Oct 26, 2026 3:48 PM ET snapshot, so any window inside that snapshot is not unseen for them. A window of messages newer than that snapshot is. Prefer --archive <old snapshot> --also-archive <newer snapshot> --split-after-archive.
  • What is reported (numbers only; no signer, room or message text): per-window score distribution (mean, median, quartiles, max, share held by the top 10), duplicate and group counts; signers ranked in both windows vs only one; Spearman rank correlation on the signers ranked in both; median rank move; top-N overlap, both over all ranked signers and over signers ranked in both, each with the overlap expected by chance; and, as a reference, how well plain message counts carry over for the same signers.
  • How to read it. Many ranked signers appear in only one window, which is expected for a room network full of one-off keys, so the common-signer numbers are the fairest comparison. A score that is less stable than plain message count means the extra rules (reply credit, reciprocity, duplicates) add noise. This is a sanity check, not proof the index measures usefulness.
  • Real-data runs write to git-ignored or outside-the-repo paths. The report itself is anonymous, but only commit a report after checking it.
